# Jev Fits Beside the Solver, Not Inside It

Where a System One classifier improves established practice in engineering, physics and mathematics. Synthesised from nine researcher notes, current to September 2026. The chemical industry is out of scope by decision of the project lead; the brief's chemistry parts are ignored.

Jev's best uses sit beside solvers, provers, rule engines and large language models, not inside their inner loops. Across all three fields the strongest usages share one shape: short technical text, a closed answer set that an existing standard or taxonomy already defines, code that owns every number, and a person or exact checker behind the answer. That shape covers maintenance and incident coding (the US Bureau of Labor Statistics' neural coder makes **39% fewer errors than manual coders** ([BLS 2017](https://www.bls.gov/iif/automated-coding/deep-neural-networks.pdf)) [V]), clause-by-clause checks against reporting guidelines and building codes (giving a model the structured PRISMA checklist lifts accuracy **from 45% to about 79%** ([Kataoka et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16707)) [V]), faithfulness screening of autoformalized theorems (a two-judge LLM consensus agrees with humans on **89.7%** of cases ([Beyond Compilation, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31002)) [V]), and gate-and-verify steps around LLM extraction and agents (a 770M-parameter checker reaches **GPT-4 accuracy at 400x lower cost** on grounded claims ([MiniCheck, EMNLP 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)) [V]). In real time, hosted Jev at 100-150 ms fits every loop of one second and up: authoring-time nudges, agent-step verification, supervisory choices at ISA-95 Level 2, conversational turns. The planned 10 ms tier opens the first machine-speed loops (100 ms budgets: inline API guards, 50-100 Hz behaviour-tree ticks, per-keystroke checks), but only with a short network path, and today's cap of **1,200 requests per minute binds search loops before latency does**. Hard real-time stays closed: PLC scans, servo loops, the LHC Level-1 trigger, tokamak disruption prediction and per-clause prover guidance run at microseconds to milliseconds on numeric data, deterministically. Functional-safety standards add a second wall: **IEC 61508 does not recommend AI above SIL 1** and ISO/IEC TR 5469 points to advisory roles under a conventional barrier. The largest gap is evidence about Jev itself: no independent public benchmark of its accuracy, calibration, tail latency or injection resistance exists, so every fit rating below is an inference from results with other models. The cheapest fixes are public benchmarks with labels: SAFARI, ProcessBench, the ISO 14224 failure-mode set, CODE-ACCORD, OSHA and NHTSA narratives, and the Beyond Compilation set.

**How to read the verification marks.** The researchers flagged every source. This report carries those flags over unchanged in meaning: **[V]** = the researcher opened the page, PDF or abstract this session; **[S]** = seen only in a search snippet or summary, numbers unconfirmed; **[M]** = cited from memory, URL or number not re-checked; **[T]** = title only; **[LR]** = low-reliability source (a vendor-adjacent blog). A citation with no mark was cited by the researcher from a search result or page without an explicit flag. All cost sums are the researchers' own arithmetic on the brief's price of $0.04 per million input tokens; token counts per item are stated assumptions. No number below was invented for this report.

## 1. Executive summary: closed answer sets, code-owned numbers, a human behind the gate

The question was where a text-only, calibrated classifier that returns Choice, Noul and Score answers, costs about $0.04 per million input tokens, answers in under 100 ms today and under 10 ms later, and holds 32k to 64k tokens of context, could improve, better automate or enhance established practice. The answer is the same in all three fields. Jev wins where an organisation already pays experts to read short technical text and pick from a fixed list, where volume or cost forced sampling, or where a brittle keyword rule stands in for a semantic judgment. It loses where the decision turns on arithmetic, dates, multi-hop inference, non-text input, adversarial text, or a deterministic deadline.

Three design rules come out of the evidence and hold everywhere. First, **code owns the numbers**: dimensionless ratios, tolerances, dates, counts and coordinates are computed by code and passed in as named buckets; Jev judges only the words. Second, **validate and calibrate each question, not the model**: every Noul, Choice or Score wording is its own classifier, and the act / escalate / abstain thresholds come from cost ratios and a few hundred labelled examples, not from trust in "trained to be calibrated". Third, **batch sibling questions into one request**: a third-party test measured about 150 ms for one question and about 150 ms for thirty questions in one call ([MindStudio benchmark blog](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/jev-vs-classic-classifiers-benchmark)) [V, LR], which makes the effective cost about 5 ms per judgment and is the only way around the 20-requests-per-second cap.

Five constraints shape every design. **(1) The request cap, not latency, binds search loops today**: 1,200 requests per minute is 20 per second, so a 10,000-node search sent one node per request takes over eight minutes; packing siblings into one request is the fix. **(2) The 10 ms tier needs a short path**: a caller 1,000 km away pays about 10 ms of round trip before routing overhead, so the tier implies regional endpoints, on-prem or edge serving, none of which is publicly announced. **(3) Assurance places Jev at advisory level**: ISO/IEC TR 5469 usage level C or A2, never A1. **(4) Zero-shot loses to a trained encoder on a narrow labelled task**: 80.1% versus 93.2% on Banking77 in the one public Jev comparison ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/jev-vs-classic-classifiers-benchmark)) [V, LR]; Jev's edge is breadth, calibration, no training, and price. **(5) Formal syntax fluency is unverified**: every usage that reads Lean, Isabelle, SMT-LIB, SystemVerilog or heavy LaTeX carries that risk.

## 2. Jev's envelope and the fit test in one page

**What goes in and what comes out.** Input is text only: a `state` (string, JSON object or array of text) plus typed questions with `instructions` and `criteria`. Output is never generated text. It is one of three typed answers, each with probabilities: **Choice** picks one option from a defined set and returns a probability per option; **Noul** returns the probability that a yes/no condition holds; **Score** returns a probability per level on an ordered, described scale. Many independent questions run over one state in one request and cannot see each other, so their outputs are marginals, not a joint distribution. The weights are the same for everyone and there is no fine-tuning; domain knowledge travels in `state`, `instructions` and `criteria`. Source: the shared brief quoting the Jev docs index ([docs.typesafe.ai/llms.txt](https://docs.typesafe.ai/llms.txt)), not re-fetched this session.

**Price, speed, size, limits.** About $0.04 per million input tokens, output free. A 1,000-token judgment costs about $0.00004, so about 24,000 judgments per dollar; one million 2,000-token documents cost about $84. Latency is under 100-150 ms per request today, under 10 ms planned. Context is 64k tokens per request, 32k for state plus the longest question. Rate limits today are 250,000 tokens per second and 1,200 requests per minute. A third-party benchmark measured about 150 ms per call, flat from one to thirty questions per call, and about 22 cents for a full Banking77 run ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/jev-vs-classic-classifiers-benchmark)) [V, LR]. No published p99 latency, jitter or long-context data exists for Jev; mean latency is not enough for any loop with a deadline.

**Known weak spots (from the brief, each with published support in section 8).** Not a calculator: weak at arithmetic, counting, numeric precision and comparing raw numbers. Weak at date and time comparison. Weak at multi-hop indirection and double negatives; it reads literally. Accuracy drops when the state is large and full of irrelevant detail. It can be steered by adversarial text inside the state. It does not generate text, code, proofs or explanations. Images, spectra, waveforms and sensor streams must first become text, named buckets or structured fields. English is strongest. The one public calibration number: on Banking77 (77 intents) Jev scored 80.1% accuracy at 88% mean confidence, overconfident by about 8 points, and temperature scaling cut the calibration error by roughly two-thirds ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/jev-vs-classic-classifiers-benchmark)) [V, LR].

**The fit test.** A usage is a good fit when most of these hold: the decision is a semantic judgment over text or structured state, not arithmetic; the answer space is closed (one of N, yes/no probability, graded level); numeric parts can be computed in code and passed in as named facts or buckets; today the task is blocked by volume, latency, cost, scarce experts or brittle keyword rules; and a wrong answer is recoverable, because low confidence escalates to a person or a reasoning model. Every table in sections 4 to 6 applies this test and reports a rating of **Strong**, **Medium** or **Speculative** with the main risk named.

**Acting on probabilities.** Decision theory gives the exact rules, valid only when the probabilities are calibrated on the task at hand. Act when p clears the cost-derived threshold C_FP / (C_FP + C_FN) ([Elkan 2001](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~elkan/rescale.pdf)) [M]. Escalate when the top probability sits in the reject band ([Chow 1970](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1970.1054406)) [M] or when a conformal set holds more than one label ([Angelopoulos & Bates 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)) [M]. Selective classification turns a moderate classifier into a high-precision one on a subset: 2% top-5 ImageNet error guaranteed with probability 99.9% at almost 60% coverage ([Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)) [V]. That result is the template for every "Jev handles X% of cases at error Y; the rest escalate" claim in this report. Calibration must be measured per question on a small labelled set (reliability diagram, ECE, Brier) and fixed, if needed, with one temperature parameter per question; contextual calibration with a content-free input raised few-shot accuracy by up to 30 points ([Zhao et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09690)) [V]. Guarantees break under distribution shift and say nothing about adversarial inputs.

## 3. Cross-cutting patterns: cascades, verifiers, search heuristics and neural predicates

### 3.1 The patterns and the evidence behind them

**Fast judge first, slow reasoner on demand.** The dual-process idea has a clear AI lineage: Booch et al. proposed fast experience-based solvers, slow deliberate solvers and a metacognitive controller ([Thinking Fast and Slow in AI, AAAI 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06002)) [V]; SOFAI showed the hybrid beats either solver alone on planning and grid navigation ([npj AI 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44387-025-00027-5)) [V]; SOFAI-LM matches or beats standalone large reasoning models at much lower inference time on graph colouring and code debugging, with no figures in the abstract ([arXiv 2508.17959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17959)) [V]; System-1.x classifies each sub-goal as easy or hard and routes it ([Saha et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14414)) [V]. In every published hybrid the fast part generates actions; Jev only selects. So Jev fits as the System 1 solver when the action space is closed, and as the metacognitive gate that decides whether to wake the slow model. The easy-versus-hard sub-goal classifier in System-1.x is exactly a Choice call.

**Cascades and routing.** Cheap-first designs are among the best-evidenced patterns in ML. FrugalGPT matches GPT-4 with up to 98% cost reduction or gains 4% accuracy at equal cost ([Chen, Zaharia, Zou 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176)) [V]. RouteLLM keeps 95% of GPT-4 quality on MT-Bench while sending 14% of queries to GPT-4, about an 85% cost cut, but only about 45% on MMLU and 35% on GSM8K ([Ong et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18665)) [V]. AutoMix cuts cost by over 50% at comparable performance ([Aggarwal et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12963)) [V]. Two 2026 warnings matter: cascades pay the cheap model before any escalation decision, and a lightweight pre-generation router beat cascade policies on four of five datasets ([Bouchard 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06350)) [V]; and learned routers collapse toward the most expensive model as budget rises because they predict scalar scores where routing needs rankings ([Lai & Ye 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03478)) [V]. A stage that costs $0.00004 and 10-150 ms nearly removes the first warning. Failure modes to design against: distribution shift breaks thresholds, and cascade accuracy is capped by the cheap stage's recall on hard cases, because a confident wrong answer never escalates.

**Verifiers and judges.** Small specialised checkers match frontier LLMs on narrow, well-defined checks and fail elsewhere. MiniCheck-FT5 (770M) reaches GPT-4 accuracy on LLM-AggreFact at 400x lower cost by checking each atomic fact against its source ([Tang, Laban, Durrett 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)) [V]. Fine-tuned judges beat GPT-4 in-domain but underperform on generalisability, fairness and adaptability; they are "task-specific classifiers" ([Huang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02839)) [V]. On ProcessBench, process reward models "typically fail to generalize to more challenging math problems beyond GSM8K and MATH" and trail general LLM critics ([Zheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559)) [V]. Short universal phrases, often five tokens or fewer, push LLM judges to the top score and transfer across judges; absolute scoring is more vulnerable than pairwise ([Raina, Liusie, Gales 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.427/)) [V]. The licensed pattern is claim-by-claim grounded checking: code splits an output into claims, and each claim plus its source becomes a Noul. Where Jev should not be the verifier: deciding whether a hard derivation step is valid. It can still check form: does the step cite a rule, does the unit label match, does the claim appear in the source.

**Weak supervision and features.** A prompted model used as many noisy labelling functions, denoised and distilled, is a working recipe: an average 19.5% error reduction on WRENCH versus zero-shot prompting ([Smith, Fries, Hancock, Bach 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02318)) [V]; 62 LLM-generated interpretable features matched 768-dimension SciBERT embeddings ([Balek et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07132)) [V]. Jev's "questions cannot see each other" matches the labelling-function abstraction, and its probabilities are soft votes. Limits: labelling functions from one model share errors, so keep non-Jev signals; numeric fields stay numeric, because text-model features do not replace raw numbers in tabular models ([arXiv 2411.01645](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.01645v1)) [V]. Distillation is the bridge to loops Jev cannot reach: Jev labels a corpus once, a tiny local model then runs at 1-10 ms on CPU or in an air-gapped plant ([Hinton et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)) [M].

**Heuristics inside search.** Search quality is bounded by budget divided by cost per node. AlphaZero searched about 80,000 positions per second against Stockfish's about 70 million and won 28-0 with 72 draws ([AlphaZero summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaZero)) [V]. In branch-and-bound, per-node inference cost is the stated bottleneck: Gasse et al. improved over expert branching rules with a GNN ([NeurIPS 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01629)) [V], and Gupta et al. had to run the GNN once at the root and a cheap MLP at every node to beat SCIP on CPU ([NeurIPS 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15212)) [V]. Tree of Thoughts reached 74% on Game of 24 at about $0.74 per case ([Yao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10601)) [V]. At Jev prices, 10,000 node evaluations with 1,000-token states cost about $0.40, below what ToT paid for a few hundred calls on one puzzle, and Jev can score all children of a node in one request. Two limits: AlphaZero's and Gasse's evaluators were trained on the task, and search exploits evaluator errors, so an exact checker (solver, proof kernel, simulator) must stay the final arbiter; and today's binding limit is the request cap, not latency.

**Neural predicates in rule engines.** "Semantic predicate in a rule engine" is DeepProbLog's neural predicate with the network swapped for a prompted classifier ([Manhaeve et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.10872)) [M]; a 2025 position paper argues the neural part should now be a prompted foundation model ([Stein et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24874)) [V]. This attacks Feigenbaum's knowledge-acquisition bottleneck from a new side: experts write rule conditions in plain language, and nobody enumerates keywords or trains a model per condition. Calibration matters most here, because MYCIN's certainty factors failed for lack of sound semantics ([Heckerman 1986](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.3419)) [M]; a calibrated Noul is a real marginal probability and can enter ProbLog, PSL or a Bayesian network as evidence. A Noul is the probability that a crisp condition holds, not a fuzzy degree of truth; Score is the closer match to a fuzzy partition. Rete-style engines assume cheap deterministic tests, so a remote predicate needs caching, batching and versioned logged outputs. Multi-step inference stays in the symbolic layer. No benchmarked system plugs a calibrated text classifier into Drools, CLIPS or a fuzzy controller; that is a novelty claim and an evidence gap.

**Semantic operators over data.** The database community has formalised the AI map-reduce role. LOTUS defines sem_filter, sem_join, sem_topk and sem_group_by with cost-based optimisation, reports up to 1,000x speed-ups on single operators with accuracy guarantees against a gold algorithm, and up to 3.6x faster full programs ([Patel et al., VLDB 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11418)) [V]. Abacus finds plans 23.6x cheaper and 4.2x faster than the next best system on BioDEX ([Russo et al., VLDB 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14661v1)) [V]. LOTUS's main optimisation is a cascade: a cheap proxy scores every row and only uncertain rows reach the expensive model, with thresholds set by sampling to meet precision and recall targets. A calibrated Noul is a better proxy than embedding similarity because its thresholds transfer across queries. sem_join is quadratic: a 10,000 x 10,000 join at 200 tokens per pair is about $800, so blocking stays essential.

For deterministic loops the route is indirect: Jev sets modes or set-point policies above the loop, or Jev labels data and a distilled local model runs inside it (8 ms on CPU in the third-party test).

### 3.3 The rate-limit ceiling

Today's limits are 250,000 tokens per second and 1,200 requests per minute. The token cap is about 21.6 billion tokens a day, or at most about $864 a day of spend; raw per-line classification of a billion log lines a day does not fit, but per-template classification does (section 4.6). The request cap is tighter for small items: one million single-document requests take about 14 hours even though the tokens would clear in about 2.2 hours, and a 10,000-node search sent one node per request takes over eight minutes. Every pairwise, rule-grid or search design below therefore packs many questions into each request: a labelling-function bank of 10-50 Nouls per item, all siblings of a search node, all clauses for one document, or all candidate pairs for one record. Robotics adds a per-account view: 20 requests per second is 20 Hz for one account, which caps a 50-100 Hz behaviour-tree tick unless conditions are batched per tick.

### 3.4 Assurance limits: IEC 61508, ISO/IEC TR 5469, the EU AI Act

Current functional-safety practice does not let an ML model be the safety function above the lowest integrity level. IEC 61508-3:2010 Annex A Table A.2 lists "artificial intelligence, fault correction" as not recommended for SIL 2 and above, with no recommendation either way at SIL 1 ([Analog Devices summary](https://ez.analog.com/ez-blogs/b/engineerzone-spotlight/posts/functional-safety-and-artificial-intelligence-268912509)) [V, via secondary source; standard text not opened]. The IET states that under IEC 61508 "AI components may not be used to directly implement safety-related applications" and that sector standards IEC 62061, ISO 13849-1 and IEC 61511 "do not currently address solutions generated by AI" ([IET 2024](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/ifbjt25i/the-application-of-artificial-intelligence-in-functional-safety-v9.pdf)) [V]. ISO/IEC TR 5469:2024 covers AI inside a safety function, non-AI safety functions protecting AI-controlled equipment, and AI used to develop safety functions ([ISO 81283](https://www.iso.org/standard/81283.html)) [V]. Its usage levels are A (AI inside a safety function at run time), B (AI in development), C (non-safety AI that could interfere) and D (no interference), with A and B split into 1 (automated decision) and 2 (no automated decision) ([ACM Computing Surveys 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3626314)) [V]; the technology classes I to III are from memory [M]. A follow-on specification, ISO/IEC TS 22440, was approved for development in 2023 with planned publication in 2026 ([IET 2024](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/ifbjt25i/the-application-of-artificial-intelligence-in-functional-safety-v9.pdf)) [V]; its September 2026 status was not checked. Ladkin argues TR 5469 treats AI parts as oracular subsystems whose outputs cannot be justified internally ([Safety-Critical Systems eJournal 2024](https://scsc.uk/journal/index.php/scsj/article/view/32)) [V, abstract].

The consequence for Jev is fixed: position it at TR 5469 usage level **C or A2**. It advises, ranks, routes or pre-screens, and a separately assured conventional function (interlock, SIS, relief device, human sign-off) remains the barrier. Avoid A1 designs. Two 2026 studies that put reasoning models above basic control kept the safety instrumented system independent and validated every action deterministically before actuation ([Alhazmi 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19819)) [V]; ([Vyas et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28011)) [V]. The IET also warns that an advisory system can raise the demand rate on the safety system if operators follow bad advice, which must enter the hazard analysis. Under the EU AI Act, AI used as a safety component in managing critical infrastructure is high-risk (Annex III), with duties on risk management, logging, human oversight and robustness; the Digital Omnibus moved Annex III duties to 2 December 2027 and product-embedded duties to 2 August 2028, with political agreement on 7 May 2026 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)) [V, legal-firm source; regulation number not checked against the Official Journal].

Practical obligations that follow: pin the model version and treat any change like an FDA predetermined-change-control modification, re-running a frozen labelled golden set for accuracy and calibration ([FDA PCCP guidance 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/04/2024-28361/marketing-submission-recommendations-for-a-predetermined-change-control-plan-for-artificial)) [V]; validate per question, not per model; log inputs, prompts, outputs, probabilities and version; make the reject band the human-oversight mechanism with measured coverage and error rates; define a degraded mode when the API is down; and treat plant data sent to a hosted API as a confidentiality and export-control question. "Same weights for everyone, no fine-tuning" helps assurance (one artefact, inspectable configuration); "hosted" hurts it (the vendor controls versions and availability). No public statement on Jev version pinning, deprecation windows, SLAs or on-prem options was found.

### 3.5 The role catalogue, extended

The brief's ten roles all have a theoretical home and an empirical analogue: triage/router (cascades, routing), screening at scale (semantic operators, selective classification), semantic predicate in a rule engine (neural predicates), heuristic inside a search loop (policy and value guidance, algorithm selection), verifier/process-reward model (NLI fact-checking, PRMs), feature extractor (weak supervision), supervisory layer over numeric control (hierarchical control, SOFAI), entity/record alignment (sem_join, cross-encoders), clause-by-clause compliance check (entailment), and select-do-not-generate (reranking). The seven researchers converged on the same additions, named differently. This report uses these names in the tables:

- **Cascade gate / metacognitive controller**: decides whether the cheap path is enough or the slow model or a person is needed; routes compute, not content.
- **Taxonomy walker**: descends a large code tree level by level with small Choice calls, with retrieval narrowing candidates (MSC, OIICS, FRA, Brick, PhySH, CWE).
- **Selective autocoder / curation-queue ranker**: auto-accept above a calibrated threshold, auto-reject far below, route the middle band; uncertainty sampling for expert time.
- **Second coder / consistency auditor**: compare a human-assigned code, flag or grade with Jev's distribution and flag large gaps (legacy CMMS labels, NBI ratings, HARA S/E/C, DQ flags versus comments, tick boxes versus free text).
- **Multi-label fan-out**: one Noul per label over one state in one request (HFACS categories, ASRS factors, Tixier's 101 attributes, MAST failure modes).
- **Applicability filter**: decide which clauses, directives or standards apply before any compliance check.
- **Interpreter of exact-check failures / post-classifier interpreter**: a CAS, statcheck, image model or numeric detector gives the verdict; Jev names what kind of failure or cause it is from the surrounding text.
- **Symbol and quantity grounding**: tell code what a symbol or number means so exact tools can run.
- **Algorithm and solver selector**: one-shot Choice over a portfolio before an expensive run (Rice 1976 framing).
- **Slow-path advisor in a fast/slow pair**: a native heuristic runs the inner loop; Jev runs rarely and re-weights it (NeuroCore, ENIGMA templates).
- **Generator-output gate**: after a conjecturer, autoformalizer or evolutionary search and before an expensive evaluator: drop duplicates, restatements and trivial items.
- **Runtime guard / action gate**: an independent cheap check of each agent action before it executes; must never be the sole barrier.
- **Authoring-time gate**: a nudge while a person writes; only possible under 100 ms.
- **Labelling-function bank and distillation teacher**: the bridge from "out of reach" loops back to Jev.
- **Cold-start prior**: text-derived probabilities as prior mean or metadata features for a recommender or Bayesian optimiser until performance data arrives.
- **Cross-document consistency audit**: align items in document A with items in document B, then ask a Noul per aligned pair (registry versus paper, requirement versus constraint, LaTeX blueprint versus Lean).

## 4. Engineering: coding, checking and routing the text that engineers already write

Engineering has the deepest evidence base of the three fields because regulators and operators have run text classifiers in production for a decade: BLS auto-codes injury narratives, Azure routes incidents with DeepTriage, arXiv classifies submissions, and NIST runs a Technical Language Processing programme for maintenance text. The common finding across those programmes is that stock NLP fails on short, jargon-heavy engineering text and that label quality and expert disagreement are the real limits ([Brundage, Sexton et al., NIST 2021](https://www.nist.gov/publications/technical-language-processing-unlocking-maintenance-knowledge)). That finding cuts both ways for Jev: no fine-tuning means the code list, definitions and examples in `instructions` must carry the domain knowledge, and the one head-to-head that exists warns that zero-shot GPT-3.5 reached only F1 0.46 on ISO 14224 failure modes against 0.80 fine-tuned ([Stewart, Hodkiewicz, Li 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08181)) [V]. But "calibration varies dramatically" across LLMs on the same maintenance logs ([Malyi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06813)) [V], and expert coders disagree with each other, so a calibrated probability that routes doubt to a person is the differentiator, more than raw accuracy.

### 4.4 Fault diagnosis, supervisory control, power systems and OT security

The expert-system tradition already splits diagnosis into numeric-to-symbolic conversion plus symbolic reasoning ([Venkatasubramanian et al. 2003, Part II](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0098135402001618)). Jev can take the symbolic half. Two 2026 papers show LLMs doing this above basic control and below the safety system: a large reasoning model kept the Tennessee Eastman plant within all hard constraints in 39 of 39 episodes against 15 failures under basic regulatory control, diagnosed 15 of 15 safety-critical faults where a scripted rule table found the root cause in 2 of 15, cost $0.07-$2.32 per campaign and had decision latency modelled at 0.05 h ([Alhazmi 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19819)) [V]; LLM agents on a batch mixer and a CSTR produced valid recovery decisions "within latency budgets compatible with the respective process dynamics", with every action "validated deterministically against interlocks, envelopes, and dynamic feasibility before any actuation" ([Vyas et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28011)) [V]. Those successes used generative reasoning models. Jev's chance is the pattern "reasoning model writes the rule set offline, Jev evaluates it online at scan rate". Jev's latency fits ISA-95 Level 2 (seconds) and Level 3 (minutes and up), not Level 0/1 loops, and OT networks segmented under IEC 62443 make a cloud-only API a deployment barrier.

### 4.5 Requirements, systems engineering and safety assurance (including automotive)

Requirements work is the cleanest fit in software-side engineering. The rules are published and closed (ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 characteristics, INCOSE rules, EARS patterns); fine-tuned BERT reaches about 90% F1 on FR/NFR; and rule-based smell tools reach only 59% precision, so a semantic judge has room. Safety assurance evidence is thin and exploratory: LLM studies on STPA and safety cases say the output needs expert review, so Jev's role is pre-screen and consistency checker, never the credited reviewer. The sharpest warning in the whole report is automotive HARA: SAFARI, an industrial benchmark of 3,000 de-identified hazardous events, shows nine frontier LLMs reach best macro-F1 of only 0.514 for Severity, 0.318 for Exposure, 0.261 for Controllability and 0.261 for the derived ASIL, and chain-of-thought raised the false-QM rate on ASIL A/B/C cases from 14.9% to 41.0% ([SAFARI, Wu et al., 17 Sept 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20584)) [V]. A graded scale that looks like a perfect Score fit can still be too hard.

### 4.7 Hardware verification and telecom operations

Regression failure triage is an accepted pain in chip verification and already uses classical ML on log signatures ([DVCon regression triage](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/1058.pdf)) [S]; that part fits Jev. A 2026 intent-based-networking paper states that light validation and conflict filtering should run inline and heavy reasoning asynchronously ([NetInspector 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21103)), which is the slot Jev fills.

### 4.11 Robotics and autonomy

This is the one engineering subfield where speed changes the design, not just the cost. SayCan already uses the language model only as a scorer over a closed skill list, combining "probabilities from a language model (representing the probability that a skill is useful for the instruction)" with "probabilities from a value function", and PaLM-SayCan reached 84% plan success and 74% execution success on 101 kitchen tasks ([say-can.github.io](https://say-can.github.io/)) [V]. Today's LLM planners answer in 1.2-1.8 s ([LLM4AD survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15281v3)) [V], so designs run them asynchronously with a deterministic fallback. Loop rates (from memory unless cited): servo about 1 kHz; motion and collision avoidance 50-100 Hz; behaviour-tree ticks 10-50 Hz; task planning and interaction 0.1-2 Hz. At 100-150 ms Jev gives a 5-10 Hz semantic layer; at 10 ms it can sit inside a 50-100 Hz tick. Cost at rate: 10 Hz x 500 tokens is about $0.72 per robot-hour, 100 Hz about $7.20. Two blockers: the request cap is 20 Hz per account, and a cloud round trip adds tens of milliseconds, so sub-10 ms only matters with on-premise or on-robot serving, which is not announced.

### 4.12 Engineering Top 5

1. **Maintenance, warranty and incident narrative coding with calibrated abstention (E4, E8, E12, E13, E20, E23, E24, E26).** Millions of records per operator, deployed precedents (BLS beats human coders; INL MIRACLE claims several staff per plant; DeepTriage since 2017), direct published benchmarks, and errors that only create review work. Public labelled sets exist to test Jev immediately.
2. **Clause-by-clause checks over code-computed facts (E76, E77, E38, E39, E37, E90).** Fifteen years of automated code checking stalled on brittle hand-written rules and dictionary tools sit at 59% precision. A Noul per clause removes the rule-authoring bottleneck, costs about $0.20 per full design pass, and can run on every save. Numbers stay in code, which side-steps Jev's main weakness.
3. **Incident routing, alert correlation and CI signal triage (E63, E64, E56, E59, E58).** Production evidence at 82.9% F1 (routing), 0.90-0.93 F1 (correlation) and 84.3% macro-F1 with 12 examples per class (CI failures). Existing systems ration LLM calls on cost; Jev removes the ration and the CVE answer sets are exactly its primitives.
4. **Agent-harness gates (E72, E73, E75).** Volume grows fastest here, the small-classifier evidence is the most direct (12 ms BERT guards, sub-0.4% router overhead), and calibration is named as the property that decides RL verifier quality. Every tool call checked, not sampled.
5. **Supervisory layer and robotics semantic loop (E30, E29, E92, E93).** The only place where 10 ms changes the architecture rather than the bill. 2026 papers show the pattern works above basic control with generative models and SayCan already uses a language model as a pure scorer; both need validation with a classifier-only model, and both must stay under a deterministic validator.

Honourable mentions: HARA consistency audit (E48) is a perfect Score shape but SAFARI shows the task is hard; alarm rationalization (E1) is universal and unproven; Brick point mapping (E18) has 85-90% published Hits@1.

### 4.13 Engineering poor fits

- **Anything inside a safety function**: SIF, interlock, machine-guard logic, live alarm suppression or shelving. IEC 61508 practice excludes AI there and network latency is not deterministic.
- **Level 0/1 control loops, servo and collision avoidance**: millisecond determinism, numeric maths, 50 Hz-1 kHz.
- **Alarm setpoints, deadbands, delay timers, rate KPIs; RPN, PFDavg, cut sets, Weibull fits, MTBF; DGA ratios, Duval zones, SPC rules; structural and code arithmetic (span-to-depth, egress distance)**: pure numeric.
- **Raw alarm-sequence similarity, sequence-of-events timing, permit expiry, AD compliance-time tracking, SLA breach, log timestamp ordering, switching orders and lockout-tagout sequences**: order, date and time comparison are named weak spots; some are also safety-critical.
- **Generating FMEA, FTA, STPA content, LegalRuleML rules, checking scripts, inspection or 8D reports, code, SVA, SysML**: Jev does not generate; even generative LLMs are unreliable on unsafe control actions.
- **Full causal-chain reconstruction from long investigation reports**: HFACS exact match of 0.18 for a tuned 8B model; ask per-category questions instead.
- **Function-level vulnerability detection and inter-procedural static-alert triage**: PrimeVul 3.09% F1 for a 7B model and near-random GPT-4; past high scores came from leakage.
- **Waveform, vibration, thermography, relay oscillography, VCD, GD&T and drawing reading, geometry-intensive BIM checks**: non-text and spatial.
- **Network configuration verification, ICD units and bit widths, coverage percentages, "at most N" rules**: address ranges, rule order, counting.
- **CVE or alert to ATT&CK technique labels as ground truth**: about 0.39 expert agreement; log text is attacker-controlled.
- **Direct ASIL assignment**: a table lookup; the S/E/C rating itself is hard (SAFARI).
- **SSVC-style decisions that need deployment context**: LLMs over-predict risk.
- **Credited certification reviewer under DO-178C or ISO 26262**: independence and tool-qualification rules; advisory only.
- **Sole defence against prompt injection, hostile commits, forged certificates, supplier-written 8Ds, counterparty contracts**: the author gains from a "pass"; one layer only, keep human sampling.
- **Non-English archives** (Dutch borehole logs, Chinese grid records): a domain-tuned model beat GPT-4 there.
- **Narrow, stable, high-volume tasks with labels**: a local fine-tuned encoder is cheaper at the margin, faster and more accurate.

## 5. Physics: after the trigger, beside the histogram, inside the shot gap

Physics splits cleanly along the numeric/text line. Everything that runs on detector data, light curves, histograms, plasma signals or spectra is a poor fit and stays with FPGAs, random forests and autoencoders. Everything that a physicist reads and sorts by hand is a strong fit: logbook entries, fault records, shifter comments, circulars, proposals, safety forms, methods sections, student explanations and manuscripts. Accelerator labs already run NLP and RAG pilots on their logbooks and report the same blockers everywhere: "highly technical" entries and heavy acronym use ([Sulc et al., DESY, Fermilab, SLAC, CERN and others, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12881)) [V]. Almost all published work is retrieval plus text generation; cheap closed-set judgment is the open gap. For theory, be strict: frontier reasoning models fail half of undergraduate problems (best 49.8% on UGPhysics ([arXiv 2502.00334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00334)) [V]) and leave research-level TPBench problems unsolved ([MLST 2025](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2632-2153/adfcb0)) [V], so a System One model cannot verify a derivation and may only do single-hop checks on one step when code owns algebra, units and numbers.

### 5.1 Control rooms, logbooks, DAQ and run control

Fermilab's logbook holds nearly one million entries since 2013, BNL derives tags from Doc2Vec topics, and CERN's AccGPT left logbooks out at first because of "potentially lower data quality"; AccGPT itself reaches 68% accuracy with Llama 3.1 ([CHEP 2024](https://www.epj-conferences.org/articles/epjconf/pdf/2025/22/epjconf_chep2025_01279.pdf)) [V]. ### 5.3 Astronomy, user facilities and proposals

Text circulars are the best-proven area. The GCN archive holds more than 40,500 human-written Circulars; an open Mistral model extracted GRB redshifts with 97.2% accuracy and RAG found 96.8% of redshift Circulars ([Sharma et al., ApJS 2026](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4365/ae2e9c)) [V]; Astro-COLIBRI turned 26,811 reports into 68,393 observations with 99.80% field-level precision and 96.5% fully correct reports, with timing and facility attribution the most error-prone fields ([Schüssler et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23270)) [V]. ESRF's proposal topic classifier reached only 56.0% proposal-level precision with reviewer agreement at Krippendorff's alpha 0.572, so the authors advise human oversight ([J. ### 5.9 Physics education and publishing

This is the best-evidenced physics area for a classifier. Physics education research already has closed taxonomies (Force Concept Inventory misconceptions, SemEval-2013 five-way labels, checklist rubrics), and 2024-2026 studies show GPT-4-class models grade at human-level agreement: GPT-4o agreed with human graders on 70-80% of partial-credit decisions with no examples or reference answers, which equals or beats human-to-human agreement, and adding explanation text to each rubric item was key ([Chen & Wan, PRPER 2025](https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.21.010126)) [V]; a fine-grained checklist rubric beat holistic scoring and agreement was weakest for mid-range partial-credit answers ([Tang, Ambrose, Cheng 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12227)) [V]. ### 5.10 Physics Top 5

1. **Gate, verify and rank around LLM extraction for materials databases (P40, P41, P42, P44).** Every pipeline already has these yes/no steps; Jev cuts their cost to tens of dollars per corpus, adds a calibrated confidence to each record, and orders the curation queue that is today's bottleneck. SuperMat's gold links give an immediate test set.
2. **Facility fault coding, recurring-fault linking, logbook tagging and RAG gating (P1, P2, P3, P6, P15, P30).** Machine availability is the main cost driver of big facilities, the text is short, the taxonomies are closed, million-entry corpora backfill for about $30, and expert review already exists as the safety net.
3. **Run-certification support and DQ audits (P11, P12, P13, P16).** The experiments themselves call the workflow manual and error-prone; the answer set is tiny; human sign-off stays; a verdict per luminosity block becomes affordable.
4. **Literature triage and circular handling (P32, P33, P34, P18, P19).** Deployed or hand-done today, closed taxonomies, daily volume, no retraining when a taxonomy changes, and two 2026 papers prove the circular task at 96-99% with generative models that Jev can do faster on the closed-set part and verify on the rest.
5. **Education grading and tutoring (P62, P60, P64).** Human-level agreement is already shown with GPT-4o; the open problems are cost, a trust signal and mid-range answers, and calibrated per-item probabilities answer all three. P64 is the one physics usage where sub-100 ms latency is the main enabler.

Runners-up: safety-form hazard screening (P26; clean fit, no evidence); simulation job triage and agent guard (P50, P51); reporting-checklist compliance (P66).

### 5.11 Physics poor fits

- **Hardware triggers (L1, FPGA, hls4ml) and high-level trigger selection**: about 4 µs at 40 MHz on numeric event data; fails twice.
- **Alert-broker classification (ALeRCE, Fink, ANTARES, Lasair, AMPEL), DQM histogram anomaly detection, Gravity Spy glitch images**: light-curve features, histograms and time-frequency images; bucketing into words would discard what the random forests and autoencoders use. Jev belongs after these tools.
- **Real-time disruption prediction, machine protection, interlocks, personnel safety systems, beam tuning and optimisation**: numeric signals inside control loops with hundreds of milliseconds of warning; deterministic and certified; Kaiser et al. show LLMs can tune but not, as recalled, beat Bayesian optimisation or RL [M].
- **Anything that compares times** ("which entry falls inside the fault window?", "was the calibration still valid?") or **reads numeric values from circulars, figures, spectra or phase diagrams unaided**.
- **Solving or end-to-end grading physics problems, verifying a full derivation or algebraic equivalence, parsing students' faulty equations**: frontier reasoning models reach 49.8% on UGPhysics; use a CAS; open LLMs fail the parsing.
- **Choosing numeric simulation settings (k-mesh, cutoff, time step) and coupled DFT settings**: numeric ML and convergence tests do this ([Digital Discovery 2026](https://pubs.rsc.org/dd/article/5/7/2968/1262935/Automatic-generation-of-input-files-with-optimised)); even frontier LLMs fail coupled settings (INCARBench).
- **Ranking symbolic-regression candidates by fit, complexity or exact units**: code does it exactly (PhySO, PG-SR).
- **Fine-grained reviewer expertise matching, scientific-merit ranking of proposals, judging novelty or importance of a paper, final accept/reject in integrity screens**: TF-IDF beat a generative model at expert finding; the text comes from an interested party; fairness rules need human judgment.
- **Cross-paper synthesis** ("do these five papers disagree about the gap symmetry?"): multi-document, multi-hop; Jev labels each paper's stance and a reasoning model combines.
- **Stoichiometry or formula equivalence in entity alignment**: arithmetic; normalise in code.

## 6. Mathematics: around the prover, not inside its inner loop

Three facts shape every rating in this chapter. Jev is not trained on any prover's data, and most published guidance models are, so a zero-shot classifier will likely lose to a fine-tuned one on the same narrow task; Jev wins on no training, new libraries, calibrated output and price. Jev's request cap is 20 per second, so search designs must be "one state, many questions", not "one request per node". And Jev's fluency with Lean, Isabelle, TPTP, SMT-LIB and heavy LaTeX is unverified, so every usage that reads formal syntax carries that risk and every usage that stays in English (back-translation compare, rubric items, PR triage) carries less. The best uses sit around the prover: faithfulness screening, corpus audits, library search and duplicates, repair routing, premise reranking. The "heuristic inside a search loop" idea holds for coarse loops (one call per goal or per problem) and fails for fine loops (per clause, per branch node): purpose-built neural clause evaluation already slows the E prover to about 6-7% of baseline speed ([ENIGMA-NG, CADE 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03182)) [V for URL; attribution of the numbers not confirmed], and Jev at 10-100 ms is orders of magnitude slower still.

Applied mathematics, statistics and education are different in kind: most of that work is already framed as closed-set classification or pair matching (MSC codes, relevance grades, duplicate pairs, checklist items, misconception lists), the volumes are large, and the field is asking for exactly what Jev outputs, priced and calibrated error rates.

### 6.1 Premise selection, tactic guidance and prover internals

Premise selection is a per-pair relevance judgment (goal, lemma) that maps directly to a Noul or Score. The proven design is two stages: a cheap retriever picks about 1,000 from 100k+, then a pair-wise reranker scores them. Magnushammer's rerank stage lifted the PISA proof rate from Sledgehammer's 38.3% to 59.5%, and Thor plus Magnushammer from 57.0% to 71.0% ([Mikuła et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04488)) [V]; LeanHammer's premise selector adapts to user context and unseen libraries and solves 21% more goals ([Zhu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07477)) [V]. Older systems (HOList, TacticToe, Tactician) predict a tactic from a closed set, which suits Jev; frontier systems since 2020 (GPT-f, HTPS, ReProver, AlphaProof, DeepSeek-Prover, Kimina, Seed-Prover) generate free-form tactics or whole proofs, where Jev cannot be the policy. AlphaProof uses RL in Lean plus tree search and, for the hardest problems, test-time RL over millions of problem variants ([Nature, Nov 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y)) [V].

### 6.2 Step verification: a Noul as process reward model

A PRM is a per-step validity probability, which is a Noul in form. ProcessBench (3,400 competition and olympiad cases with expert-labelled first wrong step) reports average F1 of 56.5 for the best small PRM and 26.6-42.1 for the others, against 87.9 for o1-mini, 71.5 for QwQ-32B-Preview and 61.9 for GPT-4o; PRMs "typically fail to generalize to more challenging math problems beyond GSM8K and MATH" (best PRM: GSM8K 68.2, MATH 62.6, OlympiadBench 50.7, Omni-MATH 44.3) ([Zheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559)) [V]. A well-trained 7B PRM reaches 0.750 with active learning ([ActPRM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10559)) [V]. For natural-language proofs, the best model scored under 25% on USAMO 2025 with failure modes of flawed logic and unjustified assumptions ([Proof or Bluff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21934)) [V], and a search summary reports that LLM graders inflate scores "by a factor of up to 20" [S, not confirmed in the abstract]. Three cheap 31B-120B generative judges matched human pass/fail on a 200-case IMO-GradingBench sample ([arXiv 2608.00004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00004)) [S]; Jev is a different class. The rule: Jev for non-numeric, local checks and as a cheap first filter; code checks arithmetic; low-confidence steps escalate; benchmark on ProcessBench before any claim.

### 6.3 Autoformalization faithfulness

The field's main open problem is semantic faithfulness, not compilation. A criterion of Lean compilation plus GPT-5.2 and Gemini-2.5-Pro consensus agrees with the human majority on 89.7% of cases (95% CI 82.1-94.3%); every system shows a compile-faithfulness gap of 3.0-29.0 points; a GPT-5.2 agent compiled 89.5% but met the semantic criterion on 60.5%; and "LLM judging is therefore useful as a human-calibrated, conservative aggregate measure, not as an equivalence oracle" ([Beyond Compilation, Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31002)) [V]. CriticLean trains critics that beat strong baselines ([Peng et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06181)) [V]; FormalAlign reports that plain LLM-as-judge had the lowest precision among compared methods and cuts evaluation from about 3 hours of expert review to under 2 minutes ([arXiv 2410.10135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10135)) [S]. An audit of five Lean benchmarks found 4,833 findings, 398 mechanically certified, with defects (missing hypotheses, simplification, wrong translation, Lean-specific hazards) that both inflate and deflate prover scores ([Ammanamanchi, Bhat, Biderman 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29493)) [V].

### 6.4 Algorithm selection: SAT/SMT/MIP, computer algebra, OR, numerical analysis

Algorithm selection is a one-shot Choice per instance with seconds to hours at stake, so latency is not the issue; the issue is input. Classical selectors use numeric features: MachSMT learns empirical hardness models on syntactic features and gains up to 198.4% PAR-2 improvement over competition winners in some logics ([Scott et al., TACAS 2021](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-72013-1_16)) [V]. New 2025-2026 work shows text adds signal: SMT-Select's embeddings of natural-language benchmark descriptions improve 7 of 9 logics on SMT-COMP 2024 ([Lu et al. 2026](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-21540-6_23)) [S, paper behind login]; LLM hidden-layer representations of combinatorial instances predict as well as traditional features ([arXiv 2512.13374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13374)) [V]. In computer algebra the published winners are trained on expression trees: a TreeLSTM beats Maple's hand-tuned integration meta-algorithm ([Barket, England, Gerhard, ICMS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14973)) [V], and pre-trained transformers beat the best CAD variable-ordering heuristics ([Jing, Zhao, Chen 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13731)) [V]. In numerical analysis, solver selection uses matrix features (Lighthouse, SALSA; a 2026 embedding on 621 matrices x 101 PETSc configurations gives 17% better top-prediction accuracy ([Liu Weng et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13255)) [V]), and DifferentialEquations.jl has switched stiff and non-stiff ODE solvers at run time since 2018 with under 5% overhead ([SciML](https://sciml.ai/news/2018/04/09/AutoSwitch/)) [V]. Jev's new contribution is to read the problem's origin in words and walk the decision trees that NAG and NIST GAMS already publish [M]; this is mostly untested.

### 6.5 Conjecturing, library hygiene and proof engineering

Generators (Graffiti, Ramanujan Machine, FunSearch, AlphaEvolve, LeanConjecturer, Lemmanaid) produce far more candidates than people can read. Truth tests are numeric and belong to code. The open gap is semantic: MathlibLemma's novelty filter rejects only duplicate declarations and statements that default `aesop` solves and "cannot detect semantic restatements of existing lemmas" ([arXiv 2602.02561](https://pith.science/paper/2602.02561)) [S]; AXLE detects duplicates by α-equivalence of types ([arXiv 2606.26442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26442)) [S]. Proof engineering is the most conventional fit: Mathlib assigns area labels by rule and suggests reviewers from an expertise file, with about 300 PRs in backlog and a median wait of about two weeks ([Growing Mathlib 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21593)) [V]; but "both LLM models and LLM agents struggle to distinguish merge-ready PRs from build-passing PRs" ([MathlibPR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07147)) [V], so Jev stays on routing.

### 6.6 Mathematical knowledge management and peer review

zbMATH and Mathematical Reviews organise abstracting by MSC codes; a supervised coarse primary-MSC classifier reaches F1 above 77% against a human baseline (zbMATH-MR agreement) of F1 81%, and using model confidence cuts manual coarse-classification effort by 86% at 81% precision ([AutoMSC, CICM 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12099)) [V]. Symlink (SemEval-2022 Task 12) links symbols to descriptions in arXiv LaTeX with over 31,000 entities; the winner used span-pair classification ([arXiv 2202.09695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09695)) [V]. For peer review, Jev can check literal single-hop properties of a manuscript; it cannot judge proof validity, and current evidence says frontier LLMs grade proofs too generously.

### 6.7 Statistics practice

The richest applied subfield. Screening, reporting-guideline checks, spin detection and test selection already have LLM studies with numbers, all closed-set judgments, and reviewers now ask for calibrated priced error rates: of 29 papers on LLM screening, only 10% reported MCC and none of the five that claimed workload savings priced false negatives ([LLM4SCREENLIT 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.12635v2)) [V]. AutoReporter's zero-shot o3-mini reached 90.09% on CONSORT and 92.07% on SPIRIT with Cohen's κ 0.70 and 0.77 ([medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.18.25326076v2); [JAMIA 2026](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article-abstract/33/3/724/8403434)) [V]; an LLM audit of 21,041 open-access RCTs found CONSORT compliance rose from 27% before 1990 to 57% after 2010 ([PMC12395317](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12395317/)) [V]; PRISMA adherence checking with a structured checklist gives 78.7-79.7% accuracy against 45.21% from the manuscript alone, with Qwen3-Max at 95.1% sensitivity but 49.3% specificity, so "reported" is over-called and human verification remains essential ([Kataoka et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16707)) [V]. statcheck found that half of NHST papers in eight psychology journals had at least one inconsistent p-value and one in eight a gross inconsistency ([Nuijten et al. 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-015-0664-2)) [V], and journals that ran it in review saw a steep decline ([Nuijten & Wicherts 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459241258945)) [V].

### 6.8 Data science plumbing

The database community already runs LLMs as yes/no matchers, type pickers and semantic filters, and its main complaint is cost: API cost forced the MatchGPT study to cut test sets to at most 1,250 pairs ([AnyMatch 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04073v1)) [V]. On unseen data, LLM entity matchers score at least 8% F1 above the best transferred fine-tuned model and GPT-4 beats the best transferred PLM by 40-68%; zero-shot ChatGPT matches RoBERTa fine-tuned on 2,000 pairs ([Peeters & Bizer](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.11244v4)) [V]. A two-phase LLM feature extractor on clinical notes improved Brier score from 0.087 to 0.036 while expected calibration error rose from 0.060 to 0.147 ([JMIR Med Inform 2025](https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e78432)) [V], which is the calibration gap a calibrated classifier targets.

### 6.9 Mathematics education

Tutoring research says step-level feedback is what works: step-based tutors reach an effect size of d = 0.76, close to human tutoring ([VanLehn 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369)) [M], with an outer loop (pick the task) and an inner loop (feedback on each step) ([VanLehn 2006](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3233/IRG-2006-16%283%2902)) [V]. LLMs solve well but tutor badly: on MathDial's about 2,800-3,000 tutoring dialogues they give wrong feedback and reveal the solution too early ([Macina et al., EMNLP 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536)) [V]. Eedi's Kaggle competition asked which misconception explains each wrong option, with separate efficiency prizes that show cost matters ([Kaggle 2024](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/eedi-mining-misconceptions-in-mathematics)) [V]. A classifier that answers in 10-100 ms and costs almost nothing can sit in the tutor's inner loop, as long as a computer algebra system owns correctness (STACK's potential response trees [M]) and a rubric or misconception list closes the answer space. Caution from the feedback literature: faster is not always better (Shute 2008 [M]); instant correction can cut productive struggle, so the design choice is when to show the judgment, not only when to compute it.

### 6.11 Mathematics Top 5

1. **Faithfulness screening and corpus audit for autoformalization (M13, M14, M15, M16).** The biggest current bottleneck in AI-for-maths data, volumes of 10^5-10^7 pairs, LLM judges already at about 90% human agreement, a closed defect taxonomy from the benchmark audit, and errors recoverable by escalation. The back-translation variant avoids the formal-syntax risk.
2. **Reporting-guideline checks and systematic-review screening (M45, M44, M46).** Direct LLM evidence (κ 0.70-0.77; structured checklists lift accuracy from 45% to about 79%), every journal submission is a customer at about one cent, and the field's open problem is exactly priced, calibrated error.
3. **Step-level verifier and misconception diagnosis in the tutor's inner loop (M61, M59, M63).** Step feedback is what works (d = 0.76), LLM tutors are too slow and unreliable, and a CAS plus Jev closes the gap at near-zero cost per student. The one place in this chapter where latency is the main enabler.
4. **Semantic-operator backend, entity resolution and the labelling-function bank (M56, M53, M57, M58).** Cost is the documented blocker; LOTUS already proves the value of a cheap proxy with guarantees; the largest raw volume of calls in the chapter.
5. **Library hygiene and proof engineering (M2, M32, M29, M33, M34).** One alignment primitive serves search reranking, duplicates and known-ness; syntactic tools provably miss semantic restatements; error-class routing cuts LLM repair calls; Mathlib has a real 300-PR backlog and simple integration.

Honourable mentions: MSC and official-statistics coding (M36, M51: AutoMSC cut effort 86% with a supervised model, and a zero-shot model removes retraining); hammer premise reranking (M1: the direct test of the search-heuristic idea, value hinging on a head-to-head against trained selectors); the hybrid PRM (M8) as the cleanest application of "code owns numerics".

### 6.12 Mathematics poor fits

- **Given-clause selection in E or Vampire; per-node branching in MIP or SAT; S-pair selection in Buchberger; Gröbner orderings; CAD variable ordering on raw polynomials**: microsecond-to-millisecond loops on numeric structure; neural guidance already costs E 93% of its speed; trained classical models win.
- **Tactic policy for frontier provers**: GPT-f, HTPS, ReProver, AlphaProof, DeepSeek-Prover, Kimina, Seed-Prover generate free-form tactics or whole proofs; Jev cannot generate.
- **Final proof-correctness verdicts, judging proof validity or finding gaps, deciding whether a theorem's hypotheses actually hold, equivalence oracle for formal statements**: Lean's kernel is exact and cheap; ProcessBench shows small verifiers fail on olympiad steps; frontier LLMs over-score as graders; even frontier judges are not equivalence oracles.
- **Arithmetic-heavy step checks, recomputing statistics (statcheck arithmetic, GRIM, effect sizes), checklist items that need counting or dates, algebraic equivalence of student answers, OEIS term matching, counterexample finding and numeric conjecture testing, hardness prediction from raw CNF or SMT**: code (CAS, Nitpick, Quickcheck, high-precision arithmetic, empirical hardness models).
- **Formula search by structure (ARQMath Task 2)**: tree matching, not prose judgment.
- **Writing formulations, hints, feedback, explanations, repairs**: Jev emits no text.
- **Knowledge tracing itself; runtime stiffness detection and spectral estimates**: numeric sequence models and numeric code with under 5% overhead.
- **Merge-readiness of Mathlib PRs**: LLM agents already fail this.
- **One flat Choice over thousands of labels (6,006 MSC codes, full SOC)**: needs shortlist or descent.
- **Item difficulty from text alone**: weak signal even for trained models.
- **Settings with a motive to game the checker** (student answers, manuscripts, proofs that assert their own correctness, tool self-descriptions): hints and triage only, never final decisions.

## 7. Real-time and latency-bound usages: what opens at 100 ms, what opens at 10 ms

This section gathers every usage from sections 4 to 6 that sits inside a loop faster than one second, plus the few that sit just outside it and would move inside at 10 ms. Three facts frame the table. First, the third-party measurement of about 150 ms per call, flat from one to thirty questions ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/jev-vs-classic-classifiers-benchmark)) [V, LR], means the effective latency per judgment is about 5 ms today when questions share a state, so batching is the first design move in every loop. Second, the request cap of 1,200 per minute is 20 per second, which is one 20 Hz loop per account, so any loop faster than that must pack all its questions for one tick into one request. Third, no p99 or jitter figure exists for Jev; every loop with a deadline needs its own tail-latency measurement, and any deterministic loop stays out regardless of the mean. The 10 ms tier only matters where the network path is short: about 2 ms within a cloud region ([Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/networking/azure-network-latency)) [V], about 10 ms round trip per 1,000 km of fibre before routing overhead, and tens of milliseconds for a typical cloud round trip from a robot or plant. No on-prem or edge option is announced.

All three need a short network path, and two of them (robots, plants) need serving on premises that is not on offer today.

### 8.2 The cheapest experiments that settle the biggest unknowns

All of these use public labelled data, cost dollars in Jev tokens, and answer a question that decides one or more chapters of this report. They are ordered by how much of the report they de-risk.

1. **Calibration per question on three domains.** Reliability diagrams, ECE and Brier for a handful of Nouls and Choices on Banking77-style intents, OSHA construction narratives ([Goh 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28865927/)) and the ISO 14224 failure-mode set ([Stewart et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08181)); fit one temperature per question and re-measure. This decides whether the act / escalate / abstain policy that every chapter relies on is usable out of the box. A few hundred labels per domain.
2. **Zero-shot versus fine-tuned coding gap.** Run Jev with rich `criteria` (code definitions plus examples) on the 62-item ISO 14224 test set and on NLBSE'24 issues ([tools page](https://nlbse2024.github.io/tools/)); report F1 against the 0.46 zero-shot and 0.80 fine-tuned figures. This decides the entire maintenance and software-triage story.
3. **ProcessBench and PRMBench F1 by subset.** One Noul per step over 3,400 cases ([ProcessBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559)); about $4 in tokens. If Jev lands with the weak PRMs (26-57), the verifier role stays limited to form and error typing; if it approaches the mid 70s, hybrid PRMs (M8) become a product.
4. **SAFARI.** 3,000 HARA cases x 3 Score questions at about $0.29 ([SAFARI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20584)). Report macro-F1 per class and the false-QM rate, and, unlike the paper, report calibration; the auditor role (E48) lives or dies here.
5. **Faithfulness agreement on the Beyond Compilation human-labelled set and CriticLeanBench** ([Beyond Compilation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31002); [CriticLean](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06181)), in two forms: direct Lean-versus-English (M13) and back-translation English-versus-English (M15). The gap between the two measures Jev's formal-syntax fluency, which no researcher could verify and which conditions a third of the mathematics chapter.
6. **CODE-ACCORD clause typing and applicability** ([Hettiarachchi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02231)) for E76 and E77, and **OSHA, NHTSA, NTSB and LERCause narratives** for E13, E21, E23 and E27, against the published classifier baselines (F1 82.22% on OSHA; 93.4% on initiating events). Public data, existing labels, direct comparison.
7. **Rerank swap-in on a LeanHammer or Magnushammer-style pipeline** ([LeanHammer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07477)) and **error-class routing on APRIL** ([APRIL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02990)): the two "heuristic inside a search loop" and "triage around the prover" claims tested head-to-head against trained selectors. Also measure sequential-call throughput against the 20-requests-per-second cap.
8. **SayCan task set with Jev in the "Say" slot** ([SayCan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01691)): plan success against the 84% reference, with the affordance product using Jev's calibrated probability. This is the single test of the robotics thesis, and it also yields the first per-step latency and cost numbers for a 5-10 Hz semantic layer.
9. **Tail latency and batching.** p50, p95 and p99 latency and jitter for 1, 10, 30 and 100 questions per request from callers in-region and 3,000 km away. Nothing in section 7 can be promised without it.
10. **Long-context and injection red-team.** Position sweeps (key evidence at start, middle, end of a 30k-token state), distractor padding, and Raina-style universal-phrase attacks on Score and pairwise Choice; also grading with student answers and supplier certificates that contain instruction-like text. Every "adversarial" risk flag in the tables depends on the result.
11. **LLM-AggreFact and WRENCH.** Jev as a grounded fact-checker against MiniCheck's 770M baseline ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)), and Jev's probabilistic Nouls as labelling functions on WRENCH against the 19.5% hard-vote result ([Smith et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02318)). These decide whether the verifier and feature-extractor roles hold at the general level.
12. **SciEntsBank and Beetle five-way grading, and Chen-and-Wan rubric items** for the education usages (P61, P62, M60), with the mid-range partial-credit band reported separately, since that is where human-AI agreement is weakest.

Open unknowns that no experiment above can settle and that the vendor must answer: version pinning and deprecation windows, an SLA, on-premises or edge serving, billing for many questions over one state, and the exact scoring rule that RLCD training optimises.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "Where can a System One model help science and engineering?" is really "where does established practice already reduce to a closed-set judgment over short technical text, and where has cost, volume or brittle rules stopped anyone from making that judgment on every item?" Read that way, the three fields converge. The regulator's coder, the alarm-rationalization team, the shifter reading DQM histograms, the curator checking a text-mined Tc, the referee checking that a hypothesis is stated, the tutor diagnosing why a step failed: each already answers a Noul, a Choice or a Score, by hand, on a sample. Jev's contribution is not intelligence but coverage with a calibrated confidence attached, and the calibration is the product: it is what turns "an 80% classifier" into "auto-accept 60% at 2% error and route the rest", which is the only form in which a regulator, a safety assessor or a journal editor can adopt it.

The second reframing concerns speed. The report set out to give real-time usages prominence, and the honest result is that the 100 ms tier already covers almost everything worth doing, because science and engineering loops that involve text run at human or agent speed. The 10 ms tier matters in three narrow places: inline machine-path guards, the robot behaviour layer and per-keystroke feedback, and all three are gated by the network path rather than by inference. Below those sit the loops that physicists and control engineers care most about, and Jev never enters them; it reaches them only by setting modes from above or by teaching a distilled local model. That is not a limitation to apologise for. It is the same division of labour that the neuro-symbolic literature, functional-safety standards and every deployed hybrid in these notes already prescribe: code and solvers own numbers, sequence and proof; the classifier owns the semantic tests between them; a person or an exact checker owns the last word.
